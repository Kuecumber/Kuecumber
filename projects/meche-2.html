4" Vent Ducted Fan

HVAC · 2026

Software
Siemens

Solo Project. 4" Ventilation Ducted Fan. As my 3D Printing Journey progresses, I am bound to start using various new filament types on the market rather than solely PLA & PETG. However, some filaments are toxic and detrimental to people's health. A simple solution is to vent out the fumes produced by melting these plastics out of the house. To transport those fumes, a ducted fan is needed. Current prices range from $ 20 to $80. Since I have excess parts, why not make my own?

Design Process

My ventilation ducted fan had 1 main job: to move fumes from one location to another. Moving fumes requires fan blades. Rotating those fan blades requires a high-powered motor. Luckily, I had an RC plane BLDC Motor left unused, as well as a 40A ESC. People online tend not to recommend printing your own fan blades because it may shatter at high RPMs. However, my motor has a low KV, higher torque, and lower RPM. Plus, I'll be powering the motor at the lower range of the recommended power ratings. Everything else would be 3D printed.

Specifications

  • Duration: June 2026, 1 Week
  • Material: PLA + PETG
  • Cost:$2 (Considering only parts bought during the project), $25 (Including ALL parts)

Outcome

More time is needed to plan out functionality and convenience. Currently, there is no power button, and power is controlled by a knob. Connecting a 2S LiPo starts the fan at 0RPM and the knob increases the PWM. But when printing toxic filaments, even with an enclosure and ventilation, you still shouldn't be inside that room. So, how would I turn that knob when the print is finished?

Process

4 days were spent creating the CAD Model. To balance the motor and not have 1 side way heavier than the other, I decided to add fan blades on both sides, with both pulling and pushing air in the same direction. There's one stator vane between the two blades, and one at the exhaust. The middle vane serves to unswirl the air flowing from the first blade to increase the efficiency of the 2nd blade to "grab" more air. The exhaust vane serves to unswirl all the air exhausted for a better flow through the ventilation pipes. The mount and ducts were inspired by modern ducted fans. The whole setup is powered by a 7-15V DC, 26A-capable battery and controlled with a PWM driver via the ESC BEC.
